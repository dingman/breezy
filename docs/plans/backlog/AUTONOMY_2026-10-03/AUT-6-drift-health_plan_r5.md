## 1. Goal state

### 1.1 Score-3 criterion (README, verbatim)

> **Score-3 criterion.**
> - Detectors cover every live family for:
>   - data freshness (tape, NBP and observation feeds)
>   - forecast and feature distribution drift
>   - calibration drift on live labels
>   - fill-rate and slippage drift
>   - venue fee and shape drift
>   - permit, process and log liveness
>   - unit health
> - Every detector maps to an action — node-local entry veto (transient conditions, auto-clearing; ARCH C5), halt, demote (through AUT-5), self-heal or alert — with delivery proven.
> - No unit fails as a matter of expected behaviour.
>
> **Live proof:** 7 consecutive days with zero unexplained failed units, plus at least one real or injected drift event per action class that produces its mapped action through the live path.

#### 3.3.2 UNKNOWN fails closed

- **NODE_LOCAL (#4, #5).** Any observation error ⇒ `UNKNOWN`. For at most `WATCH_TICK_STALE_S` (180 s) it vetoes nothing; past that it returns its own veto reason, AUT-1's writer records `DetectorEvent(state=UNKNOWN, detail=persistent)`, and the transition sends a CRITICAL `aut6_detector_unknown_persistent`. It clears on the next good observation.
- **VERDICT detectors (r4).** An unreadable input gives `outcome=INCONCLUSIVE` with `metrics.unknown_reason="input_unreadable"` and `metrics.unknown_streak=k` (a metric, not an `assumptions` tag: that enum is closed). INCONCLUSIVE never counts as PASS. At `k ≥ unknown_streak_max` the producer writes #25 FAIL naming the detector and sends a CRITICAL; the liveness detectors #6, #7, #18, #29 and #30 themselves report FAIL `reason=blind`. A detector with nothing to evaluate writes `INCONCLUSIVE` with `metrics.day_status=NO_INPUT`, never a missing verdict (C4 Invariants, U10).
- **Unit-health reads.** A non-zero exit, timeout or D-Bus error from `systemctl`/`journalctl` makes the pass `UNKNOWN`, never "zero failures". It increments `passes_unknown_streak`, leaves the heartbeat's `ts_ns` unchanged (§3.11) and feeds #25. A persistently UNKNOWN health unit therefore fails #29 (streak ≥ 3 or age) and the AUT-5 dead-man's ARCH age rule (> 1800 s) within about 30 min; it can never read as healthy.
- **Fold.** An unreadable fold (resolver raises, chain verify fails, export prefix mismatch) or an **empty fold** (the venue chain holds zero rows, impossible after AUT-5a's bootstrap) is never "no subjects". The producer writes a CRITICAL `fold_unreadable` through §3.6, writes `fold_ok=false, fold_reason=<unreadable|empty>` into its heartbeat, and writes no subject verdicts; #26 FAILs on `fold_ok=false` at the next health pass. A non-empty fold with no CHAMPION, HALTED or CHALLENGER (all RETIRED, for example after the KILL) is the legitimate `_host/` case, with `fold_ok=true`.
- **Fold unreadable in the health pass (r5, F4).** The health unit also needs the fold to pick subjects. When its own read fails (same causes), it writes #26 FAIL under `~/.local/share/breezy/derived/verdicts/_host/` with `metrics.fold_reason`, sends a CRITICAL `fold_unreadable` directly through `deliver_with_proof` (writer `health`; it does not wait for the intraday heartbeat), and records the pass as `pass_result=UNKNOWN`: `passes_unknown_streak` increments and the heartbeat's `ts_ns` stays unchanged (§3.11), so #29 and AUT-5's dead-man also fire if the fold stays unreadable.

### 3.4 Producers (C4 writers)

#### 3.4.1 Common rules (r4 conforms the identity and validity rules to C4)

- Output `~/.local/share/breezy/derived/verdicts/<family_id>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<verdict_id>.json` (0600 file, 0700 dir), `verdict/v1`, written atomically and never rewritten.
- **Identity (C4, P4-11).** `verdict_id` = sha256 of the canonical body **minus `verdict_id` and `produced_at_ns`**. `valid_until_ns` is **slot-anchored**: the producer's timer slot start (the 5-min or 10-min firing instant, or the daily 05:30Z/13:00Z slot) plus the validity, so a recompute in the same slot dedupes and the store refuses a different body under an existing id (`test_differing_body_same_id_refused`).
- `producer_code_sha` ∈ `pins.PRODUCER_SOURCE_SHA256[producer_id]` for `aut6.intraday`, `aut6.daily` and `aut6.health` (P6-13); each producer computes its closure hash at start and refuses to run if unpinned.
- `assumptions` only from the closed C4 enum: AUT-6 uses `no_policy_ruling` and `drill`, nothing else.
- Subjects: every family the fold names CHAMPION, HALTED or CHALLENGER per venue. Host-wide detectors (#6, #18, #19, #21–#31) take the venue champion as subject; with none (non-empty fold), they write `~/.local/share/breezy/derived/verdicts/_host/`, which the engine ignores.
- **Validity (W1, Z4):** intraday and health verdicts `valid_until_ns = slot_start_ns + 8 h` (`INTRADAY_VALIDITY_S = 28800` = `ATTEST_VERDICT_VALIDITY_H`); daily `+ 26 h`; the producer refuses above `MAX_VERDICT_VALIDITY_H`.
- **Refresh:** write on outcome change, or when the newest verdict for `(subject, detector)` is older than `INTRADAY_REFRESH_S = 3600` (= `INTRADAY_ATTEST_VERDICT_PERIOD_MIN` 60).
- **ATTEST invariant (ARCH §4.5 W1, in ARCH's names):** `ATTEST_PERIOD_H + L_max + ATTEST_MARGIN_H ≤ ATTEST_VERDICT_VALIDITY_H` with `L_max` = 60 min + 150 s ⇒ 6 + 1.04 + 0.5 ≤ 8. AUT-6's side: at every instant the newest PASS per (subject, intraday HEALTH detector) has ≥ 28800 − 3600 − 300 − 130 = 24770 s of validity (the 130 s is the most a slot start can precede `produced_at_ns`), which is ≥ 21600 + 300 s. Pinned by `test_intraday_refresh_keeps_attest_gap_free`; the schedule row goes to AUT-5's `test_attest_cadence_has_no_expiry_gap`.
- #6 reports `PASS window=closed` outside its window and `PASS reason=family_halted` while halted (§3.13).

#### 3.4.6 `aut6.fill_better_than_ask` (#15) in full

- **Population.** Every C1 `LifecycleEvent(event=FILLED)` in the 36 h lookback whose `client_order_id` joins (C1 invariant ii) to an `OrderLink` with `time_in_force == IOC` and to an entry `DecisionRecord(kind=TrySubmit)`. **Excluded:** resting orders (`time_in_force ∈ {GTC, GTD, DAY}` or anything ≠ IOC: a resting bid legitimately fills below the current ask), exit orders (client ids carrying an `src/breezy/persistence/exit_tags.py` prefix), `source=canary`. Drill and voided-pair fills are included (W12). An unjoinable fill is not judged here; it is AUT-1's join-gap `HEALTH` FAIL.
- **Leg normalisation.** The leg comes from the instrument id (`leg_of`, composite NO-leg ids, G24). The C1 fill `px` is the Nautilus instrument price (the exec client books NO fills via `instrument_price_for_leg`). If a record instead carries the venue's representation of a NO buy, `(ORDER_SIDE_SELL, ORDER_INTENT_BUY_SHORT)` per `VENUE_SIDE_FOR_LEG`/`VENUE_INTENT_FOR_LEG`, its wire price is converted with `instrument_price_for_leg("no", px)`. If the price space cannot be decided from the record (memory note `venue-represents-no-buy-as-sell-buy-short`), the fill is `INCONCLUSIVE reason=leg_price_space_undecidable`, never FAIL.
- **Book at the fill, not the decision ask.** `ref_ask = min(decision ask from the C1 `depth_ref` Depth10 payload, every bought-leg best ask in catalog Depth10 frames with ts in [eval_ns, fill ts_ns])`, read with one native `order_book_depth10(instrument_ids=[id], start=eval_ns − 60 s, end=fill_ts + 5 s)` call. For a NO leg without its own depth rows, the NO ask is `1 − best YES bid` of the paired YES instrument via `instrument_price_for_leg` (WP6 verify-first records which form the catalog holds). Same-timestamp book updates are applied before the quote derived from them (L-25 rule).
- **Rule.** `FAIL` iff `fill_px < ref_ask − 0.001`. A legitimate ask drop between decision and fill lowers `ref_ask`, so the fill matches a displayed level and PASSes, with `metrics.ask_drop_between_decision_and_fill=true`.
- **Missing book.** No Depth10 frame covering `[eval_ns, fill_ts]` (ingest lag or gap) ⇒ that fill is `pending_depth`; the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE only if a pending fill is older than 6 h (`unknown_streak_max = 72` passes), then #25 CRITICAL. Pending fills are re-evaluated every pass inside the 36 h lookback.
- **Frame gap (r5, F9).** If `max_frame_gap_ns`, the widest interval between consecutive Depth10 frames for the bought leg inside [eval_ns, fill_ts], exceeds `FILL_FRAME_GAP_MAX_NS = 2_000_000_000` (2 s), the book in that interval is not known well enough to call a fill better than every displayed ask: the fill is `INCONCLUSIVE reason=frame_gap` and never FAIL, so it can never HALT the venue. Such fills count toward `metrics.inconclusive_frame_gap`; they are not pending (no later ingest can close a recorded gap) and do not feed the #25 streak, but a day with any is listed in the WP9 report for review.
- **Latency (r4).** Fill → depth in the catalog (ingest every 15 min; measured catalog staleness up to 1769 s on a healthy host, r2) → next producer pass (≤ 5 min), which writes the verdict **and** the `integrity_floor` demand file → watch-actor tick (≤ 60 s): **≈ 36 min worst case on a healthy ingest**, independent of the engine. The engine HALT row follows within 150 s of the producer. A stalled ingest extends detection until #25 fires at 6 h; the repeated-defect exposure in that time is bounded by `rung_net_position_held`, the per-position cap and the venue daily budget (operator-owned values, never read).
- **Remaining false-positive risk, stated.** (i) A venue fill at a price inside the spread that never appeared in a Depth10 frame (hidden or very short-lived liquidity between two 10-level snapshots) would FAIL; no such case is known on this venue, and the action is a HALT on an INTEGRITY freeze, which fails closed. (ii) A recorder-vs-node clock skew > 5 s could exclude the matching frame; the window pads −60 s/+5 s and records `metrics.max_frame_gap_ns`, and a gap over 2 s is INCONCLUSIVE (r5, F9), which also narrows risk (i) to sub-2 s liquidity. (iii) A mis-joined `client_order_id` is impossible by C1 invariant (ii) but would surface as AUT-1's join gap first. **Clearing:** the INTEGRITY HALT freezes the venue; it clears only through build-side incident handling (ARCH W15): root cause evidenced under `docs/incident-reports/`, peer-reviewed, then cleared through the halt CLI and the registry CLI on the C5 API. Never an operator decision, never automatic.
- **Memory.** ≤ 5 fills a day, one bounded single-instrument query each; WP6 verify-first measures the ΔRSS of one query on the largest instrument-day in a fresh child (L-49 amendment) and requires it inside the 512M unit cap with 2× margin.

### 3.6 Delivery proof and the ALERT executor (r4: ARCH §4.6, V1, V3, U3, R9.2-Z4, erratum E-1)

#### 3.6.1 API

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class DeliveryProof:
    delivered: bool
    status_class: Literal["2xx", "3xx", "4xx", "5xx", "transport", "not_configured", "outbox_overflow"]
    event: str
    ts_ns: int
    recorded: bool

def deliver_with_proof(sink: AlertSink, payload: AlertPayload, *, writer: str,
                       records: DeliveryRecordWriter,
                       attempt_kind: Literal["alert", "retry", "canary", "drain"],
                       drill: bool = False,
                       now_ns: Callable[[], int] = time.time_ns) -> DeliveryProof: ...
```

- Calls `emit` **directly on the webhook branch** (via `TeeAlertSink.sinks`), never through `emit_alert` or the tee, because both swallow failure (G25); every other branch receives the payload through `emit_alert` first. Proof is an HTTP 2xx. `httpx.HTTPStatusError` maps to `status_code // 100` (3xx included); other `httpx`/`ssl`/`OSError` errors map to `transport`. Only the exception type is logged. Per-attempt hard timeout `ALERT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT_S = 5` (ceiling ≤ 10).
- **`attempt_kind` (r4, ARCH enum):** `alert` (the first attempt by the originating process, including `breezy-check-alerts`), `retry` (a later attempt by the same claimant), `canary`, `drain` (an attempt on an entry claimed from the outbox by a drainer). r3's `inline`, `redeliver` and `check` map to `alert`, `drain` and `alert` (writer `check`).

#### 3.6.2 Per-attempt records (ARCH §4.6, P6-6, V3)

- One write-once file per attempt: `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<ts_ns>_<writer>_<d|f>.json` (`d` delivered, `f` failed), 0600 in 0700 directories, written `mkstemp` then `os.link` (never replaces an existing name; a same-name collision retries at `ts_ns + 1`), file and directory fsynced.
- **Fields:** ARCH's `event`, `ts_ns`, `delivered`, `status_class`, `severity`, `attempt_kind`, `drill`, plus `schema: "alert_delivery/v1"` (common rule) and two diagnostic fields, `site` and `outbox_entry` (the entry's basename, no path). No URL, message, detail or exception text. The C5 reader uses only ARCH's fields.
- **Writer ids** (one per process type; L-50): `node`, `intraday`, `daily`, `health`, `redeliver`, `canary`, `check`, `deadman`, `engine`, `probe`, and `legacy-<component>` for the migrated oneshot sites.
- **Record write failure:** never blocks delivery; logs `alert_delivery_journal_unwritable` at ERROR (type only), increments the process counter printed in the unit summary line as `journal_write_failures=<n>`, and returns `recorded=False`. #23 reads both. Because #5 counts only records, an unwritable record root also ends in the node veto after 26 h: fail closed.

#### 3.6.3 Durable outbox, claim and reclaim (r4: ARCH V1, U3, R9.2-Z4; **erratum E-1**)

- **Scope (V3).** Every proof-bearing alert: every CRITICAL from any site, and every alert of any severity emitted for a C6 detector action or a named failure mode, from any area. `PROOF_BEARING_EVENTS` (literal, in `src/breezy/runtime/alert_delivery.py`) holds the detector-action and failure-mode event names; the gate test derives the detector half from `CATALOG` so a new detector cannot be missed (`test_detector_and_failure_mode_alerts_use_delivery_proof`). Other WARNING and INFO lines keep `emit_alert`.
- **Entry.** Before any HTTP attempt the originating process writes `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/<ts_ns>_<event>.json` (0600, schema `alert_outbox/v1`, the `AlertPayload` fields plus `severity`, `drill`, `writer`), via `mkstemp`, file `fsync`, `os.link` to the final name (no replace; a collision retries at `ts_ns + 1`), then directory `fsync`. Write-once.
- **Claim (E-1 order, binding).** A drainer claims an entry in two steps: **first** `os.utime(entry)` stamps the claim time on the entry; **then** `os.rename(entry, ~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/claimed/<drainer>/<name>)` claims it, and the directory is fsynced. The losing renamer gets `ENOENT` and never sends. **Never rename then utime:** a crash between those two would leave a claimed entry carrying its old mtime, which a second drainer would reclaim at once while the first might still send.
  - A crash after the `utime` and before the `rename` leaves the entry unclaimed with a fresh mtime. It stays drainable, because **entry age is read from the filename `ts_ns`, never from the mtime**; only claim age reads the mtime.
- **Attempt and completion.** The claimant attempts delivery under `ALERT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT_S`, writes the delivery record, and only then removes the claimed file and fsyncs the directory. Before each retry it re-stamps its claimed path with `os.utime`; `ENOENT` there means another drainer reclaimed it, so it stops without sending. Delivery is at-least-once: a duplicate page is possible, a lost CRITICAL is not.
- **Reclaim.** A drainer may reclaim any `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/claimed/<other>/<name>` whose mtime is older than `ALERT_CLAIM_STALE_S = 60` (ceiling ≥ 3 × `ALERT_DELIVERY_TIMEOUT_S` = 15), with the same order: `utime`, then `rename` into its own `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/claimed/<drainer>/`. Each reclaim increments `metrics.reclaims` in the drain summary; more than 3 for one entry is a #23 FAIL.
- **Drainers.**
  - The originating oneshot claims its own entry immediately (drainer id = its writer id) and attempts it once (`attempt_kind=alert`).
  - The node worker (§3.6.4) claims node entries, retries every 20 s while it holds the claim (`retry`), and drains leftovers at boot (`drain`).
  - `breezy-autonomy-alert-redeliver` (AUT-6, every 5 min) claims unclaimed entries whose filename age is ≥ `REDELIVER_MIN_AGE_S = 60` (an originator that died before claiming) and stale claims (`drain`).
  - AUT-5's dead-man drains entries older than `ALERT_OUTBOX_STALE_S = 300` (ceiling ≤ 300) by the same `drain_outbox(drainer="deadman")` call (ARCH §4.6, §5.2 dead-man row).
- **Bound.** `ALERT_OUTBOX_MAX = 128` files in `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/` plus `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/claimed/` (ceiling ≤ 256). **16 of those slots are reserved for `severity=CRITICAL` (r5, F5):** `ALERT_OUTBOX_CRITICAL_RESERVED = 16` (a `Final` in `src/breezy/runtime/alert_delivery.py`, asserted `< ALERT_OUTBOX_MAX`), so a non-CRITICAL entry is refused once the count reaches `ALERT_OUTBOX_MAX − 16 = 112`, and a CRITICAL is refused only at 128. A WARN storm therefore cannot crowd out a CRITICAL. Past the bound for its severity, the payload is not queued; a `delivered=false`, `status_class=outbox_overflow` record is written and #23 FAILs.
- **Abandonment.** An entry whose filename age exceeds 24 h is still attempted, and #23 FAILs naming it (`abandoned`).
- **One-writer row** (`test_autonomy_files_have_one_writer`, U3): `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/alerts/outbox/*.json` → write-once (no lock) → creators {node, intraday, daily, health, canary, check, deadman, engine, probe, legacy-*}; rename claim → {each originator for its own entry, node worker, redeliver, deadman}; remove → the current claimant only.
- **Write-on-change is not consumed by a failed send:** a producer's transition alert counts as sent only on `delivered=true`; otherwise the outbox carries it and the next verdict keeps `metrics.alert_pending=true`.
- **Outbox write failure:** logs `alert_outbox_unwritable`, writes a record with `outbox_write_failed=true` when records are writable, increments `outbox_write_failures=<n>`, and still attempts the delivery once; #23 FAILs.

### 3.9 Unit health: sources, ordering, scope, ownership, timers, "unexplained"

- **Failure source of truth: the journal.** Each pass reads `journalctl --user -o json --after-cursor=<cursor> MESSAGE_ID=d9b373ed55a64feb8242e02dbe79a49c` (argv list) and takes `USER_UNIT`, `USER_INVOCATION_ID`, `UNIT_RESULT`, `__REALTIME_TIMESTAMP` and `__CURSOR`.

- **Commit order (G5).** For each entry, in journal order:
  1. **Classification record** `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/unit_health/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<unit>__<USER_INVOCATION_ID>__class.json`, created `O_CREAT|O_EXCL` (0444). If it exists, it is reused: dedupe is on `USER_INVOCATION_ID`, never on time.

  3. **Action record** `…__action.json` (`O_CREAT|O_EXCL`) citing the selfheal record or the delivery record.
  4. Only after every entry of the batch has both records: rewrite `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/unit_health/seen/<unit>.json` (one file per unit, single writer under the health lock), then the cursor `~/.local/share/breezy/evidence/unit_health/cursor.json` (`os.replace`), **cursor last**.

#### 3.10.1 The memory check (#31, r4: ARCH §5.2 V14, U6)

**Studies-flock holders (r5, F11)** are identified by a static scan, not a text grep: for each `deploy/systemd/*.service`, the `ExecStart=` argv and every `deploy/systemd/*.sh` it names are parsed with comments stripped, and a unit is a holder iff a non-comment command takes `breezy-studies.lock` (`flock`, `fcntl`). In-process takers (today `breezy-autonomy-producer-daily`, §3.4.4) are listed in the literal `IN_PROCESS_STUDIES_LOCK_UNITS`, kept honest by an AST test that every `src/` module opening `breezy-studies.lock` maps to a listed unit. **`MemoryMax=infinity`** (or an absent limit, which `systemctl show` reports as `infinity`) is parsed as +∞, never skipped: it FAILs condition 1 for a holder, condition 4 for an own-lock unit and condition 6 for the sum.

Each run reads the **effective** `MemoryMax`, `MemoryHigh` and `MemoryPeak` (`systemctl --user show`, drop-ins included) of every `deploy/systemd/` unit and `MemTotal`, `MemAvailable` from `/proc/meminfo`. FAIL, each condition named in `metrics.violations`:
1. a studies-flock holder with `MemoryMax` > 14G or `MemoryHigh` > 12G, unless it carries a named raised cap;
2. a named raised cap > 16G (`tests/unit/test_analysis_units_memory_capped.py:138`), or one whose timer slot falls inside `[16:30Z, 01:15Z)` or shares its slot with another heavy (> 4G) unit;
3. a scheduled study whose last `MemoryPeak` reached its `MemoryHigh` (ARCH: no silent OOM-kill; it is FAILed by name);
4. own-lock autonomy units (every area's) with a summed `MemoryMax` > 4G;
5. quote-tape-ingest, named while its temporary drop-in stands (ARCH counts its effective limit; today 12G/14G per §5.2, 14G found 10-03);
6. **the memory sum** `max(studies-holder MemoryMax) + Σ own-lock MemoryMax + ingest MemoryMax + measured node RSS + measured recorder RSS` > `MemAvailable_free`, where `MemAvailable_free = MemAvailable + Σ MemoryCurrent of those same units + node RSS + recorder RSS` (memory already resident in the counted processes is added back, so it is not counted twice). This implements ARCH's "sum of effective limits plus measured node and recorder RSS against `MemAvailable`" (`test_health_memory_sum_within_memavailable`); the add-back is AUT-6's reading of an ARCH sentence that does not say whether the node is running when `MemAvailable` is read (observation O-2, §11). **The `MemAvailable` side is the 24 h minimum of the health pass samples (§3.9, r5 F10)**, so a single daily read cannot miss the night's tightest point.
7. a heavy (> 4G) unit with a timer firing in `[01:00Z, 04:30Z)`.

### 3.11 Monitor the monitors and the dead-man (r4: ARCH §4.6, P6-5, P6-13)
